# Treasurer principles

Rules that shape how `02_Reconcile` and `04_Report` do their job. Not general commentary — only what changes how a reconciliation or a report gets built.

## Record-keeping
- Every dollar in and every dollar out must be recorded and reconciled — no exceptions for small amounts.
- One home per transaction: date, description, category (chart of accounts), amount, source document, reconciled status. Don't duplicate a fact across files — link to the ledger row instead of re-typing it.
- Keep digital copies of source documents (receipts, invoices, bank exports); back them up off the local machine.

## Reconciliation (`02_Reconcile`)
- Match every bank statement line to a ledger entry and a receipt/invoice where one exists. Anything that doesn't match is a **discrepancy** — flag it, don't silently drop or force-fit it.
- Common discrepancy causes worth checking before escalating: uncashed/outstanding cheques, timing differences (deposit in transit), bank fees not yet entered, duplicate entries.
- The person who reconciles should not be the same person who received/banked the cash, where the organisation has enough people to separate the roles. Note who did the reconciliation.
- A reconciliation is only "done" when the adjusted book balance equals the adjusted bank balance — if they don't match, the difference must be found, not explained away.
- Nothing gets posted to the ledger (`03_Ledger`) until a human has resolved every flagged discrepancy for that period.

## Controls (apply across the pipeline)
- No cash payments except petty cash; everything else by bank transfer, card, or cheque, with a receipt/invoice on file.
- Payments over an agreed threshold need two authorised sign-offs.
- The person who authorises a purchase shouldn't be the person who receives the goods or reconciles the transaction.
- Never sign a blank cheque or pre-approve an amount-less transaction.

## Reporting (`04_Report`)
A board report should let the board answer, at a glance: are we solvent now, are we tracking to budget, and what changed since last time. Include:
- Statement of Financial Position (balance sheet) — assets, liabilities, net assets, as of the report date.
- Statement of Financial Performance (income/expenditure vs. budget) for the period and year-to-date, with dollar and percentage variance.
- Cash flow summary — enough to answer "can we pay what's coming due."
- Plain-language commentary on any variance past the board's agreed threshold (e.g. 5–10%) — the *why*, not just the number.
- Current bank account balances as of the report date.

Write for the least financially confident person at the table — a number without a sentence explaining what moved it is not yet a report.

## Solvency check (every report)
- Can the organisation pay its debts as they fall due, right now and over the reporting horizon?
- Are current liabilities greater than current assets? If so, why, and is that expected to reverse?
- Is income timing reliable, or is there a risk of a cash gap even though the annual position looks fine?
