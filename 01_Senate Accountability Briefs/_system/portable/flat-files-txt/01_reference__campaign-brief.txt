Hi everyone,  

I am asking disability leaders to urgently use the Senate Accountability Briefs to engage senators before the NDIS Amendment Bill is voted on. 

Please share with all the people in our community.

The background is simple. The Bill is expected to pass. Labor, the Coalition and One Nation together hold around 61 Senate votes, well above the simple majority needed. The Greens, minor parties and independents do not have the numbers to stop it.

The House of Representatives has already passed the Bill on the voices, meaning there was no recorded vote. That matters because people can avoid individual accountability when their name is not attached to the decision.

That cannot happen in the Senate.

If this legislation leads to increased isolation, family stress, carer burnout, service closures, reduced participation or higher long-term costs, the blame will not sit with the disability community. It will sit with the senators who had the power to ask harder questions, demand safeguards and protect the people they represent.

This is why the briefs matter. They make the impact local. They connect the Bill to each senator’s state, electorate, reputation and responsibility. National numbers are easy to ignore. Local consequences are harder to dismiss.

What we need to do now is clear. Send the briefs, comms and post to all senators expected to support the Bill. Ask them what safeguards they will secure before voting yes. Ask them how they will monitor harm after passage. Ask them what responsibility they will accept if implementation hurts participants, families, carers and providers in their own communities.

This is not about attacking senators. It is about making sure responsibility lands where power sits.

Feel free to address and respectfully engage the senate how you wish, however be as creative with your approach with the data provided. Your personal story is essential to change these numbers to be actually human impact.

If people with disability, families and carers are expected to live with the consequences of this decision, senators must be expected to own those consequences too.

Vote expected by 14 August 2026!
